OatFi had a real product and no way to show it. Their lending infrastructure lived inside API documentation that nobody outside engineering could parse, so deals kept falling through. As the lead designer, I worked closely with the PM and founders to translate all of that into something a business team could actually use and present in a room.

## The Challenge

The product exposed technically complex lending capabilities but had no usable interface for non-technical business users. Sales cycles required heavy explanation, and demonstrating the product's value meant pulling in engineers every time.

## How We Approached It

I had several working sessions with the PM to understand what each product needed and how we could make it simpler to grasp. We had an API document with technical specifications as our starting point. The goal, together with the team, was to translate all of that into something more human and digestible.

## Prototypes Built for the Sales Room

Once we validated information with subject matter experts, we built interactive prototypes. I made about 4 of these, drawing from competitive research and stakeholder interviews and walkthroughs. We didn't have the budget or time for end user research, so we worked with what we had and moved fast.

The founders took those prototypes directly into client meetings. They worked. OatFi used them to bring on more than 10 new business partners.

I also contributed to a user journey map that helped the team align on product features and expected behaviors before anything went into development.

## A Modular UI Clients Could Actually Embed

When we designed the flagship BNPL experience, the team needed something clients could drop into their own platforms without heavy engineering work. I structured it as modular side panels that could be embedded directly, which reduced the integration effort on the client side and gave the team a reusable pattern to build on for future product lines.

The onboarding flow was another area where we simplified meaningfully. What had been roughly 10 steps came down to around 5, making the documentation upload process feel a lot less overwhelming for new clients going through verification.

## Branding That Gave the Sales Team Something to Stand Behind

In parallel with the product work, I helped evolve OatFi's visual identity for digital channels and designed 4 whitepapers for the sales team. These turned dense technical and financial content into clear visuals, giving the team consistent and professional material that felt aligned with the product experience they were selling.

## Results

- More than 10 enterprise clients were closed using the interactive prototypes
- Engineering was no longer needed in sales conversations to explain the product
- The onboarding flow went from roughly 10 steps to 5
- The modular UI system became the foundation for future product lines
- The sales team had 4 whitepapers and a refreshed visual identity to work with

The work went beyond delivering screens. The team left with a product experience, a set of standards, and tools that kept supporting OatFi's growth after my involvement ended.
